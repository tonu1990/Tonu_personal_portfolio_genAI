### Master's Research Thesis

Tonu's MSc research thesis was titled **"A Comparative Study of Pedestrian Detection Bias between Indian and European Conditions Using CNN Based Pedestrian Detectors."**

The research investigated fairness and geographical bias in computer vision systems, particularly examining differences in pedestrian detection performance between Indian and European conditions.

The research highlighted the importance of evaluating AI models across diverse environments rather than assuming that successful performance in one geographical or environmental setting guarantees comparable results elsewhere.

This work contributed to Tonu's understanding of computer vision, deep learning, AI fairness, bias evaluation, and responsible AI development.

### Additional Academic Projects

During his MSc programme, Tonu also worked on academic projects involving face similarity analysis and time-series forecasting.

One project, **"Exploring Trade-offs Between Black-Box and Glass-Box Models in Face Similarity: Siamese Networks vs. KNN,"** compared different modelling approaches for facial similarity analysis. A poster associated with this work was published through ACM.

Another academic project, **"A Comparative Study of Pedestrian Footfall Trends Using Time Series Forecasting,"** investigated forecasting approaches using Holt-Winters, SARIMA, and Prophet.

Together, these projects expanded Tonu's experience across computer vision, machine learning, model comparison, statistical analysis, and predictive analytics.

### M.Tech Research

During his M.Tech studies, Tonu conducted research titled **"Hiding Images and Video inside Video using Message Dependent Key Based Encryption and Entropy Based Hiding."**

The research explored techniques for securely concealing digital information within video content through a combination of encryption and steganography.

The proposed approach involved encrypting secret images or video data using a message-dependent encryption technique and subsequently embedding the encrypted information into a high-resolution cover video using local entropy-based hiding.

The research aimed to improve information security and payload utilization in video-based data hiding.

A paper associated with this research was published in IIJC, Volume 4, Issue 3, in July 2014.

Tonu's M.Tech education and research provided an early foundation for his later interest in Computer Vision, Machine Learning, and Artificial Intelligence.
